# Copy rules

Editorial adaptation by Team Pickles, 2026. See the packaged NOTICE for attribution. This repository owns the canonical C01–C26 rules. Apply to articles, marketing, website copy and related content; C20–C25 apply when their interface, domain, localization or publication surfaces are present. Keep the requested language and the author's actual meaning.

## Meaning and evidence

### C01 [MUST] Know the reader and the action
Determine who is reading, what they already know, and what they need next. Match a button, product description, campaign page, and long article to their different jobs. If the required source text is missing, ask for it rather than inventing a rewrite.

### C02 [MUST] Preserve facts and real voice
Keep the author's position, uncertainty, humor, vocabulary, and useful idiosyncrasies. Fix the smallest passage that causes the problem. Do not manufacture personal anecdotes, opinions, customer speech, or intentional mistakes to make text appear human.

### C03 [MUST] Earn every factual claim
Use approved facts and substantiated numbers. Never invent conversion lifts, awards, client logos, response times, stock counts, integrations, credentials, testimonials, research, or guarantees. If evidence is missing, omit the claim, request the fact, or label demo content visibly. A precise-looking invented number is still false.

### C04 [DEFAULT] Start where the information starts
Remove generic scene-setting about the modern world, rapid change, or the importance of the topic. Keep a story or personal opening when it actually supplies context or voice. Do not force every paragraph into the same conclusion-first structure.

### C05 [DEFAULT] Describe the mechanism or benefit specifically
Explain what the product does, for whom, and under which conditions. Replace abstract promises with known actions, limitations, examples, or consequences. Do not add an invented statistic just because specificity would improve the sentence.

### C06 [DEFAULT] Cut text that fits any business
Read a claim without the company name. If it could describe an unrelated business unchanged, identify the missing mechanism, subject, or evidence. Replace it with something useful or remove it. Keep simple conventional labels when their universality helps navigation.

## Language and cadence

### C07 [DEFAULT] Prefer familiar verbs
Use direct verbs and concrete nouns. Review promotional uses of elevate, empower, unlock, revolutionize, seamless, cutting-edge, robust, leverage, streamline, and next-generation. These are review cues, not a ban on legitimate technical terminology or a quoted phrase.

### C08 [DEFAULT] Remove Russian promotional filler
Review phrases such as «индивидуальный подход», «комплексные решения», «выводим на новый уровень», «уникальная экосистема», «максимальная эффективность», «инновационный», «безупречное качество». State the actual service, process, condition, or evidence. Do not merely replace one abstract adjective with another.

### C09 [DEFAULT] State the intended point directly
Avoid rehearsed oppositions such as “Not just a website, a growth engine” or «Это не просто сайт, это…». Keep a contrast when the reader must distinguish real alternatives. Do not invent an inferior alternative to make the preferred option sound better.

### C10 [DEFAULT] Remove theatrical introductions
Cut empty confessions, faux-exclusive insights, dramatic colon reveals, self-answered questions, and “what nobody tells you” setups. Keep real questions in interviews, FAQs, and explanatory teaching when they serve the format.

### C11 [DEFAULT] Replace importance claims with information
Remove sentences that merely label something pivotal, remarkable, powerful, or a testament to excellence. Explain the event, feature, consequence, or evidence that made the claim relevant.

### C12 [DEFAULT] Remove commentary about the commentary
Delete redundant cues telling readers that a point matters, that they should notice it, or that the next point is key. Retain an explanation when the implication is not apparent. Avoid trailing phrases that pretend to analyze a fact without adding a consequence.

### C13 [MUST] Attribute borrowed authority accurately
Do not write “experts agree” or «исследования показывают» without a real supporting source. Preserve citations and qualifications when editing research-based content. Do not attach a source that supports a different claim.

### C14 [DEFAULT] Keep the clear noun
Use consistent terminology for the same product, role, and action. Avoid rotating synonyms just for variety. Simple “is,” “has,” and «делает» often explain more than grandiose role descriptions.

### C15 [DEFAULT] Let sentence length follow the thought
Remove repetitive slogan triplets, identical paragraph templates, negative lists, and manufactured punchy fragments. Keep clear longer sentences and intentional fragments that belong to the author. Do not mechanically alternate short and long sentences or flatten every paragraph into equal length.

### C16 [DEFAULT] Cut empty hedging without deleting uncertainty
Remove qualifiers that do no work. Preserve “may,” “usually,” “I think,” «возможно», and other qualifications when the evidence or author's position requires them. Do not turn a cautious statement into an unsupported guarantee.

### C17 [DEFAULT] End on useful information
Avoid a repeated summary, an inspirational closing metaphor, a fake revelation, or a stock “in conclusion.” End on the last concrete point or a useful next action. Summaries remain appropriate when requested or needed for a long reference document.

## Formatting and microcopy

### C18 [DEFAULT] Keep punctuation functional
Do not use decorative dot chains or dashes as layout devices in labels and promotional headlines. In English short copy, prefer sentences and straightforward punctuation over a rhetorical em-dash habit. Preserve grammatically required Russian dashes, dialogue, date ranges, proper spelling, and exact quotations. Never run a blind character-replacement pass.

### C19 [DEFAULT] Make formatting serve reading
Use headings for real sections, lists for parallel items or steps, and tables for comparison. Avoid a heading over every two sentences, bold emphasis scattered through prose, and emoji-decorated headings. Sentence case is the default for UI labels unless brand or language conventions differ.

### C20 [MUST] Make action labels and outcomes agree
Name what happens: send a request, choose a size, view delivery terms, save changes. Avoid ambiguous “Submit,” “Go,” and «Узнать больше» when a specific destination is available. Do not promise an immediate purchase if the action only opens a conversation.

### C21 [MUST] Explain states in the user's terms
Loading text says what is happening. Errors say what went wrong and how to recover. Empty states explain the absence and a useful next action. Success text says what completed and what follows, using only known timing and contact details. Keep internal stack names and debugging messages out of visitor flows.

### C22 [MUST] Keep information meaningful to the domain
Use category-specific descriptions, units, filters, and specifications. A board length, shoe size, and equipment capacity are different concepts. Do not fill a characteristics tab with identifiers while omitting the facts needed to choose. Do not infer specifications from a product photo.

### C23 [MUST] Localize meaning and presentation
Maintain a glossary for repeated controls, colors, categories, and status terms. Check plurals, date and currency formats, units, phone formatting, script coverage, and longer strings. Avoid accidental language mixing and raw supplier labels. A language switch should retain the current item or equivalent page when possible.

### C24 [MUST] Keep conditions near the decision
Place price basis, exclusions, preorder terms, delivery limits, consent, and required commitments where they affect the action. Do not hide a material condition in a remote footer. Do not rewrite approved legal language as part of cosmetic editing.

### C25 [DEFAULT] Audit the full visible surface
Read headlines, navigation, captions, badges, product cards, alt text, tooltips, forms, empty states, and footer. Check for broken grammar, strange metaphors, duplicate ideas, ungrounded specificity, and placeholders. A convincing headline cannot repair nonsensical small print.

### C26 [MUST] Separate critique from authorship claims
For an audit, identify the actual phrase, the problem, and a small fix. Do not declare who wrote it or assign an AI probability. For an edit, return the complete revised text with a short explanation of material changes when useful. Do not rewrite untouched sections just to show activity.
